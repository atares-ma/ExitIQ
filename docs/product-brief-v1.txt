Prepared for the atares development team | 4 November 2026 | Version 1.0

1 Product decision

Build ExitIQ as a guided discovery journey followed by an adaptive
technical assessment. Give users useful observations before requesting
extensive preparation data, then help them turn unanswered questions
into a practical work plan. Preserve tactical depth across finance, tax,
legal matters, governance and process execution.

The product must support owner or family businesses, corporate
subsidiaries, PE-backed businesses and VC-backed businesses, including
combinations. Separate ownership, management, transaction objective and
the sale perimeter. Use one explicit scoring profile and independently
activated modules.

Launch recommendation: show three readiness dimensions: Strategic
clarity, Operational transferability and Transaction preparation. Show
answer coverage and evidence support alongside each. Keep critical risks
outside the numeric score. Do not display an overall exit score,
valuation, sale probability or implied multiple uplift.

Model status: all weights, answer anchors, coverage thresholds and
priority rules in this document are proposed product parameters for
version 1. They are expert design assumptions to calibrate with atares
transaction professionals and users. They are not empirically validated
buyer benchmarks.

Scope and deliverables

-   A staged user journey, English example copy, profiling fields and
    deterministic routing rules.

-   Eight discovery screens and a catalogue of 55 scored controls, with
    answer points, local weights, applicability and suggested
    contributors.

-   Context-specific domain weights, treatment of unknown answers,
    evidence and contradictions, plus worked calculations.

-   Risk escalation, tax scope, report generation, permissions,
    analytics, acceptance tests and release priorities.

Document map

Sections 2–5 cover experience and routing. Sections 6–8 specify scoring
and risk rules. Section 9 defines the discovery journey. Sections 10–17
contain the question catalogue. Sections 18–22 cover implementation,
outputs, testing, calibration and sources.

This is a new implementation recommendation. Findings from the 2
November product test explain the priorities, including inconsistent
question counts, weak progress visibility and an incomplete generated
report; current production behaviour has not been re-tested for this
specification.

Suggested entry copy

See your business through a buyer’s eyes.
Explore what makes your business attractive, what may be difficult to
transfer and what needs preparation. Start with a few questions, then
choose whether to explore the details.
Button: Start my first assessment

Only promise a completion time after observing real users on the
relevant route. Display the computed number of applicable controls;
update it transparently when a new answer activates a module. Explain
that additional questions have been added and why.

Interaction rules

-   Use one assessed fact per question. Put explanatory terminology
    below the question or behind a clearly labelled help control.

-   Show neutral progress, for example “Finance: 3 of 6 questions”.
    Avoid “Step 4 of 4” for a long final question block.

-   Use explicit options for Unknown, Needs internal confirmation, Needs
    adviser review and Prefer not to answer. These are unresolved
    statuses, not numeric failures.

-   Save progress on every answer. Permit backward navigation and show
    changes to applicability before hiding previous answers.

-   Give one short observation after a theme, not praise or warnings
    after every answer. Base it on recorded responses.

-   Offer “Continue preparation” and “Save and return” at the discovery
    boundary. Contact, report delivery and newsletters remain separate
    optional actions.

Technical presentation

Keep the main question, short answer options and navigation visible on
common mobile screens where possible. Use radio buttons for single
choice and checkboxes only for genuine multiple choice. Give controls
visible keyboard focus and sufficient contrast. Test on real mobile
devices; the previous review covered a desktop browser only.

Early liquidity screen

Before the discovery sequence, ask an unscored question: Are there known
or expected difficulties meeting payment obligations during the next six
months? Options: No known pressure; Potential pressure requiring review;
Confirmed acute pressure; Unknown. Potential or unknown pressure creates
a review task. Confirmed acute pressure activates the specialist
pathway; the tool does not itself diagnose insolvency. Obtain a
qualified assessment for that conclusion.

Choosing the primary profile

Ask: “Which perspective best describes the transaction you are
preparing?” Explain the four choices. Suggest CORPORATE for a subsidiary
disposal by an operating group, PE for a sponsor-led exit, VC for a
venture-backed liquidity event, and OWNER for a private or family-led
exit. The user confirms the choice. A personal holding company alone
does not make a business CORPORATE.

For mixed ownership, retain all tags and ask which perspective should
drive the weighting. If undecided, allow discovery and show that scoring
is pending profile confirmation. Do not silently choose a profile.
Recalculate visibly if it changes, retaining the previous version for
comparison.

Example: a founder-led PE portfolio business may use the PE weighting
profile while retaining founder-dependency questions. A PE-owned
carve-out activates separation controls even though CORPORATE is not the
primary profile.

Unknown triggers and route precedence

A trigger answered UNKNOWN opens a short clarification and leaves the
associated module pending. Keep its controls in the applicable
denominator until applicability is resolved. Record “applicability
unresolved”; do not auto-hide it or silently treat it as N/A. Pending
branches prevent a final “complete” assessment.

If “exploration” is selected, assess preparation of alternatives rather
than demanding an approved sale decision. IPO and distress activate an
early specialist pathway: preserve progress, show relevant preparatory
observations, and withhold a standard full-readiness conclusion until an
appropriate route is supported. A confirmed acute liquidity threat takes
priority over the usual discovery order. Ordinary losses alone do not
establish distress.

The route count is computed, not hard-coded. CORE has 37 controls;
special routes add up to 18. Discovery reuses four controls. This is a
technical assessment that can be completed across sessions. User testing
should identify removable duplication before any short-duration promise
is made.

Shared questions with context specific wording

Q12 measures continuity of decision authority in every business. For
owner-led management use “You are unavailable for four weeks. Which
decisions wait?” For employed management use “A key executive is
unavailable for four weeks. Which decisions wait?” Keep the same answer
anchors and score semantics.

Q07 measures understanding of repeatable business economics. For
established profitable businesses ask about normalised earnings and cash
conversion. For deliberately loss-making growth businesses ask about
reconciled cohort/unit economics and the documented path to sustainable
economics. Do not award points merely for profitability or a stated
growth plan.

Q32 covers legal ownership. Its venture wording explicitly refers to the
fully diluted cap table. Q38 assesses distribution rights; Q51 assesses
converting instruments. These are distinct controls and must not
duplicate a generic “cap table quality” score.

Illustrative route combinations

-   Family-owned IT services, full share sale, no debt: OWNER + CORE.
    Add founder wording, rather than adding a second founder score.

-   Industrial group disposing of a dependent subsidiary: CORPORATE +
    CORE + SEPARATION, with ASSET_TRANSFER only if assets/business
    transfer is actually proposed.

-   Founder-led PE buy-and-build exit with leverage and MIP: PE + CORE +
    DEBT + PE_CONTEXT + EMPLOYEE_EQUITY + INVESTOR_RIGHTS; acquisition
    history activates Q18.

-   VC-backed SaaS partial exit with new capital, options and
    convertible notes: VC + CORE + INVESTOR_RIGHTS + EMPLOYEE_EQUITY +
    CONVERTIBLE + PARTIAL + PRIMARY_CAPITAL. Add CROSS_BORDER only when
    supported by facts.

Before scoring, show a readable configuration summary: selected
perspective, proposed transaction, active modules and unresolved routing
questions. Let the user correct it.

Rationale

OWNER emphasises ownership alignment and continuity beyond the owner.
CORPORATE allocates more operational weight to systems and execution
capacity and more transaction weight to process readiness. PE gives more
strategic weight to options and the demonstrated future plan and more
operational weight to business economics. VC emphasises stakeholder
alignment and legal/economic rights while avoiding an EBITDA-only view.

Local weights and overlays

Within a domain, local weight 3 denotes a central control, 2 a
supporting control and 1 an enabling control. The catalogue specifies
each value. There are no hidden multipliers. Conditional controls join
their stated domain and share its existing weight; they do not increase
the dimension total above 100.

A triggered tax module does not reduce tax importance elsewhere: the
universal tax controls always apply. Corporate separation adds a
specific tax separation control Q49. Multiple ownership tags activate
relevant questions but never average or multiply the four profile
matrices.

Do not reweight on the basis of a bad answer, respondent role or
sales-lead attractiveness. Horizon changes task urgency, not readiness
arithmetic. Store profile, question catalogue and scoring version with
every result.

7 Scoring coverage and evidence

Deterministic calculation

For a dimension, let p_d be its domain percentage. Let A_d contain all
applicable controls, including unresolved answers and pending
applicability. Let u_q be each control’s local weight. Assign effective
weight e_q = p_d × u_q / sum(u_j for j in A_d). Effective weights
therefore sum to 100 for a fully applicable dimension.

A substantive answer a_q is exactly 0, 50 or 100, using the
question-specific anchors. Let K be the controls with substantive,
non-conflicted answers. Answer coverage C = sum(e_q for q in K) / 100.
The answered-only index R = sum(e_q × a_q for q in K) / sum(e_q for q in
K). If K is empty, R is null, never zero.

Also calculate the missing-answer range: L = sum(e_q × a_q for q in K) /
100; U = L + sum(e_q for applicable controls outside K). L and U are
points on the 0–100 scale. They are mathematical missing-answer bounds,
not a statistical confidence interval.

Display and completeness rules

-   Discovery: observations only. Never publish a complete dimension
    score from the discovery sample.

-   Full assessment: show a rounded dimension index only if weighted
    dimension coverage is at least 80% and each domain has at least 50%
    local-weight coverage. Otherwise show “Insufficient coverage” and
    the completion required.

-   At 80–99% coverage label the index “Indicative, incomplete”; display
    C and the missing-answer range. A complete assessment requires 100%
    substantive coverage of applicable controls, no pending
    applicability or conflicting answers, and a confirmed profile.

-   A complete questionnaire can still contain low scores or confirmed
    issues. “Complete” describes the data, not approval to transact.
    Outstanding critical risks produce a separate status even when every
    answer is known.

Worked example

In one domain, three controls have local weights 3, 2 and 1. Answers are
100, 50 and UNKNOWN. Answered-only domain index = (3×100 + 2×50)/5 = 80.
Coverage = 5/6 = 83.3%. Missing-answer range = 66.7–83.3. Answering
UNKNOWN cannot produce a complete high-readiness result. If the third
control is confirmed absent, it scores 0 and the domain result becomes
66.7 at 100% coverage.

Evidence support

Keep evidence separate from answer points. Set evidence factor v_q to 0
for self-report only, 0.5 for a dated evidence reference with named
responsible role, and 1 only after an authorised reviewer has actually
reviewed the support. An upload or filename alone never earns 1.
Evidence support = sum(e_q × v_q)/100 across applicable controls;
unknown answers have v_q = 0. Label this metric “Evidence support”, not
confidence or verification of the entire business.

Resolution and review

Low numeric anchors create preparation tasks. A critical flag
additionally requires the stated factual predicate or authorised
specialist judgement. UNKNOWN creates “Needs review”, not a confirmed
legal, tax or solvency defect. Critical flags are not automatically
cleared by high scores elsewhere. Record resolution evidence, reviewer,
date and reason.

Examples of contradictions: “all material decisions delegated” versus
“all approvals require the founder”; “no convertibles” versus an
outstanding note disclosed later; a fully standalone assertion versus
essential unplanned parent services. Reconfirm the facts instead of
selecting the more optimistic answer.

Priority engine

Sort confirmed critical prerequisites first, unresolved critical facts
second, and ordinary preparation gaps third. Within each class rank by
planned deadline, then impact. For a known numeric gap use impact = e_q
× (100−a_q)/100. Show dimension and domain because effective weights
belong to different dimensions and must not be presented as one
aggregate score. Use effort only as an additional planning field, not a
reason to hide a high-impact issue.

Tasks carry source question IDs, reason, suggested responsible role,
suggested timing relative to the transaction, dependency, evidence
required and status. Dates proposed by the tool are planning
suggestions, not statements of legal deadlines. Only a maintained
jurisdiction rule or adviser confirmation may supply an actual legal
deadline.

Initial picture

Show at most three observations linked to actual answers. Example: “You
identified personal customer relationships as a strength. Your
continuity answers suggest checking how these relationships can be
maintained after a change of ownership.” Where answers do not support an
observation, say that further detail is needed.

Do not infer automatic fragility from low committed revenue in a project
business, or declare a customer concentration unacceptable from one
percentage. Those facts direct Q09 and Q10. Do not estimate valuation,
tax savings or a multiple effect.

Transition into technical depth

Suggested copy: “Your first answers highlight the areas worth exploring.
Continue to review the supporting figures, ownership arrangements, tax
preparation and practical steps. You can pause or ask a colleague to
help.”

Use six visible chapters: What makes the business valuable; How
dependable the business is; What transfers to a new owner; What supports
the next stage; What can be demonstrated; What the transaction should
achieve. The scoring domains remain separate from this editorial order.

Branch and diagnostic rules

ACQUISITIONS is active if a material acquisition creates ongoing
integration obligations; its trigger is collected with company history.
Add Q18 to O4 and recalculate its local denominator. A corporate
divestment is not automatically an acquisition for this purpose. Unknown
acquisition status leaves applicability pending.

For Q16, the 30% discovery scenario is illustrative. The technical
follow-up uses a relevant demand scenario for the business, not a
universal growth target. For Q17, a known serious security issue may
require a specialist task but is not automatically a numerical
transaction veto.

Q19 measures whether the proposition can be supported. It is not an
independent judgement that a strong moat exists. Raw market-growth,
retention, recurring-revenue and concentration statistics may inform

Answer states

SUBSTANTIVE stores a scored option. UNKNOWN,
NEEDS_INTERNAL_CONFIRMATION, NEEDS_ADVISER_REVIEW, WITHHELD and
CONFLICTED are unresolved and carry no points. NOT_APPLICABLE_REQUESTED
remains unresolved until a rule or authorised reviewer confirms
NOT_APPLICABLE_CONFIRMED. Confirmed N/A removes local weight; an
explanation and provenance are mandatory. A whole domain with no
applicable controls requires an approved model exception; do not
silently redistribute its profile weight.

INACTIVE answers remain stored for audit and are excluded from
calculation. If a route reactivates, ask the user to reconfirm old
answers. Never delete answers when a user edits a context field.
Preserve both contributor submissions when they conflict; designate a
responsible resolver.

State sequence

CONTEXT → DISCOVERY → INITIAL_PICTURE → DEEP_ASSESSMENT → RESULT. SAVED
can resume any stage. SPECIALIST_PATH and APPLICABILITY_PENDING can
interrupt the sequence. Result status is data_complete or
data_incomplete plus a separate risk status. All transitions must be
resumable and versioned.

Recalculation order: validate context → resolve applicable controls →
detect contradictions → compute domain denominators/effective weights →
compute coverage/indices/bounds/evidence → apply risk rules → generate
tasks → render deterministic result → optionally generate narrative. The
language model cannot change scores, routes or risk status.

20 Narrative governance and collaboration

Generation contract

Generate prose only from a validated result object containing answer
IDs, computed metrics, permitted evidence summaries and approved action
templates. The report must distinguish “You reported”, “This may imply”
and “Suggested next step”. It must not describe self-reported documents
as independently verified.

-   Prohibit unsupported valuation estimates, multiple uplifts,
    buyer-wide assertions, tax savings, legal conclusions and invented
    market benchmarks.

-   Do not label 70% a market buyer expectation. If internal bands are
    used, label them as model-defined preparation levels and publish
    their definitions.

-   Validate required sections, referenced IDs, score consistency and
    complete sentence endings before publication. Reject and fall back
    to a deterministic template if generation is truncated or
    inconsistent.

-   Render the deterministic result immediately. Narrative generation
    may run asynchronously with progress and a retry path. Keep
    completed answers safe on failure.

Permissions and contribution

Default access is private to the assessment owner. A respondent role is
descriptive and does not grant access. Inviting a CFO, legal adviser or
tax adviser requires explicit selection of recipient and permitted
module scope. Give participants only the information necessary for their
assigned work. Maintain an audit trail and permit revocation.

Seller-level tax records require a separately scoped area. Provide
aggregate status to the main assessment where permitted; do not reveal
private seller circumstances or conclusions to management or other
shareholders by default. Users may record a status or adviser
confirmation without uploading source documents.

Data and lead handling

Explain storage, AI processing, retention and deletion before collecting
substantive data. Match wording to actual implementation and the
approved privacy notice. Do not promise anonymity if answers are stored
against an identifiable session or later linked to contact data.

Assessment completion, report delivery, adviser contact and marketing
opt-in are distinct actions. Do not automatically open a contact form
over the result. Offer an optional review of identified gaps after the
user has seen useful findings.

Analytics

Record stage views, starts, theme completion, route changes, saves,
resumes, result views and optional contact requests using minimal event
metadata. Exclude answer text, tax details, evidence links and document
content. Segment by approved broad context, channel and device where
appropriate. Deduplicate internal tests and obtain the required privacy
review before instrumentation.

Calibration before release

Use at least four expert-reviewed archetypes, including a
mixed-ownership case and a separation case. Have atares professionals
score the same scenarios independently, compare disagreements, and
revise anchors before tuning weights. Test threshold sensitivity and
whether profile changes create unreasonable jumps. Run user sessions
across owners, CFOs, corporate M&A and investors. There is no promised
conversion uplift until observed in real use.

Pilot discovery-to-depth continuation, assessment completion,
saves/resumes, unresolved rates and qualified follow-up conversations.
Evaluate commercial lead quality separately from readiness scores. Keep
the question and scoring version fixed during each comparison period.

A production scoring route is available only when all its applicable
required controls are implemented. If a supported context is not yet
fully implemented, provide discovery and a clearly identified specialist
pathway rather than a partial full-readiness score.

Decisions to approve before coding the scoring engine

Product owner: approve the staged journey, absence of a single aggregate
score, supported transaction scope and role-based wording. M&A content
owner: approve all 55 anchors, domain allocations, profile matrices,
critical predicates and task language. Tax/legal owners: approve their
module content, jurisdiction routing and privacy boundaries.
Engineering: approve versioning, state transitions, permissions and test
fixtures. These are internal implementation sign-offs, not a need to ask
end users for repeated confirmation during an assessment.

Tax scope and sources

The tax module assesses preparation. It does not calculate liability,
recommend a holding structure or determine whether a particular tax
treatment applies. Germany is an illustrative jurisdiction, not the
default for all users. For unsupported jurisdictions, retain generic
preparation questions and show that local advice is required; do not
claim local-law coverage.

In Germany, share disposal treatment can differ depending on the seller
and circumstances, including provisions in section 17 EStG and section
8b KStG. Certain contributions/share exchanges can have seven-year
subsequent-taxation consequences under section 22 UmwStG. The relevant
transaction history therefore needs adviser review. No automatic
seven-year rule should be applied to every restructuring.

Definition of done

A development handover is complete when each route has an owner, every
